How to Brief an SEO Agency: A Checklist for UK Online Shops

16 November 2026 · by Jamie Irwin, Straight Up Search

Published by Straight Up Search, an eCommerce SEO and generative engine optimisation (GEO) agency serving Northampton, Northamptonshire and online shops across the UK. Written by Jamie Irwin, Founder and SEO Director.

Most SEO engagements that go wrong were set up wrong. The agency guessed at the goal, the shop owner assumed things were included that weren't, and three months later nobody agrees on whether it's working.

A good brief fixes that before any money changes hands. Here is the checklist we'd want every UK online shop to fill in before speaking to any SEO agency, including us.

1. The commercial goal, in one sentence

"More traffic" is not a goal. Write the outcome you'd actually pay for:

  • "Grow non-brand organic revenue from our top 5 categories by 30% in 12 months."
  • "Get our new own-brand range ranking for its product-type searches before Christmas."
  • "Stop the organic decline that started after our replatform."

An agency that can't explain how its plan connects to your sentence is the wrong agency.

2. Your numbers today

Share read-only access, or at least screenshots, of:

  • Google Search Console – clicks, impressions and top queries for the last 16 months.
  • Analytics – organic sessions, conversion rate and revenue by landing page.
  • Your platform – Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento or custom, and any planned migration.
  • Brand vs non-brand split – if you know it. If you don't, ask the agency to work it out first, because it changes what "growth" means.

3. What you sell and who buys it

List your top categories by revenue and margin, your best-selling products, and the categories you want to grow. Note any seasonality. An agency should prioritise where margin and search demand overlap, not where it's easiest to rank.

4. Competitors you actually lose sales to

Not the biggest names in your sector – the three to five shops that customers compare you with. Include marketplaces if Amazon or eBay listings outrank you for your own products.

5. What has been tried before

Previous agencies, past link building, content you've published, migrations, penalties or traffic drops, with rough dates. This saves weeks of detective work and stops anyone repeating a tactic that already failed.

6. Your team and your capacity

Who can make changes to the site, how quickly, and who signs off content? If developer time is scarce, say so. A brilliant technical roadmap is worthless if nothing gets shipped.

7. What's in and out of scope

Be explicit about whether you expect the agency to cover:

  • Technical SEO fixes (or just recommendations)
  • Category and product copy
  • Blog and buying-guide content
  • Digital PR and link building
  • AI search visibility (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity)
  • Reporting cadence and format

8. How you'll judge success

Agree the reporting metrics up front: non-brand clicks, organic revenue, rankings for a fixed set of commercial terms and, increasingly, how often AI assistants mention you. Agree what "on track" looks like at 3, 6 and 12 months.

Questions to ask any agency you brief

  1. What would you do in the first 30 days, and why that first?
  2. How do you separate brand and non-brand performance in reports?
  3. Who will actually do the work, and how many clients do they look after?
  4. What's the notice period, and who owns the content and links if we part ways?
  5. What would make you tell us SEO isn't the right channel for us right now?

A simple brief template

Section What to include
Goal One sentence, commercial outcome, timeframe
Baseline Search Console, analytics, platform, brand/non-brand split
Products Top categories by revenue and margin, growth priorities
Competitors 3–5 real rivals, plus marketplaces
History Past agencies, tactics, migrations, drops
Capacity Who can change the site, and how fast
Scope Technical, content, PR, AI visibility, reporting
Success Metrics and milestones at 3, 6 and 12 months
