What Is Entity SEO? A Guide for UK Online Shops

16 November 2026 · by Jamie Irwin, Straight Up Search

Published by Straight Up Search, an eCommerce SEO and generative engine optimisation (GEO) agency serving Northampton, Northamptonshire and online shops across the UK. Written by Jamie Irwin, Founder and SEO Director.

Entity SEO is the work of making sure search engines and AI assistants know exactly who your business is, what it sells and which other things it is connected to. Keywords tell Google what a page is about. Entities tell Google what your business is.

For a UK online shop, that difference matters more every year. Google's Knowledge Graph, AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity all work from a model of things and the relationships between them. If your shop is a clear, well-described thing in that model, you are easier to rank, easier to recommend and harder to confuse with someone else.

What counts as an entity?

An entity is any distinct, identifiable thing: a person, a company, a brand, a product, a place or a concept. For an online shop, the entities that matter most are:

  • Your organisation – the legal company and the trading name customers know.
  • Your brand(s) – especially if you make or own a product range.
  • Your products – each with a stable name, brand, GTIN or MPN where one exists, and a price in GBP.
  • Your people – usually the founder, plus any named experts behind your buying guides.
  • Your service area or locations – for most online shops, "United Kingdom", plus a town or county if you have local customers.

Why entity SEO matters for online shops

  1. Brand searches resolve to you. When someone types your shop name, Google can show a knowledge panel, sitelinks and your own profiles instead of marketplace listings or a similarly named business.
  2. AI assistants describe you accurately. Generative answers lean on consistent facts. If your name, category and location differ across the web, the assistant hedges or leaves you out.
  3. Products are matched correctly. Clear product entities (brand + identifier + offer) help Merchant Center, rich results and comparison answers line up with the right page.
  4. Trust signals add up. Reviews, press mentions and directory listings only help if Google can be sure they are about you.

A practical entity SEO checklist for UK online shops

1. Decide on one canonical set of facts

Write down your exact business name, trading name, website, contact email, phone format, founding year and service area. Use the same version everywhere. Small differences ("Ltd" vs no "Ltd", an old phone number, a previous address) are the most common reason entity signals split.

2. Add Organization schema to your homepage

Mark up your homepage with Organization (or OnlineStore) structured data that includes name, url, logo, description, areaServed and a sameAs list pointing to your official profiles. If you are a service-area or online-only business, you can leave out a street address entirely.

3. Build an About page that reads like a fact sheet

Who founded the shop, when, what you sell, who you sell to and where you ship. Link the founder to their own profile pages. AI assistants quote About pages more often than most shop owners expect.

4. Give every product a clear identity

Use Product schema with name, brand, gtin or mpn, sku, image, and an Offer with price, priceCurrency set to GBP and availability. Make sure what the schema says matches what the shopper sees on the page.

5. Claim and align your official profiles

Google Business Profile (if eligible), LinkedIn company page, social accounts, Companies House and a small number of reputable directories. Each should carry the same name, description and website link, and each should be listed in your sameAs.

6. Earn mentions that name you in context

Press coverage, supplier pages, guest articles and podcast appearances that mention your shop alongside your category ("UK online shop for…") teach search engines what you are known for.

7. Check what AI assistants already say

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode "Who is [your shop]?" and "Best places to buy [your category] in the UK". Note any wrong facts and trace them back to the source pages, then fix those first.

Common entity SEO mistakes we see

  • Old addresses or phone numbers left on directories after a move.
  • Different brand names on the website, Google Business Profile and social accounts.
  • Product schema copied from a theme with placeholder brand names.
  • No founder or team information anywhere on the site.
  • sameAs links pointing to profiles that no longer exist.

Frequently asked questions

Is entity SEO different from technical SEO? It overlaps. Structured data and site architecture are technical tasks, but entity SEO also covers off-site consistency, profiles and mentions.

Do I need a Wikipedia page? No. Most UK online shops never will. Consistent structured data, a strong About page and well-aligned profiles do most of the work.

How long does it take to see results? Schema and profile fixes are usually picked up within weeks. Knowledge panels and AI answers can take a few months to reflect the change.
